# Competitor problem claims: what they say they SOLVE

Research date: 23 Sep 2026. Method: direct fetch of homepages plus 1 to 3 product, solution or industry pages per competitor (WebFetch, curl for it.conex). WebSearch quota was exhausted, so page discovery used known URLs, on-page link lists and Bing.

**Read this first (evidence quality):**
- Most quotes below come through WebFetch, which returns a model-made extract of the page. Short phrases in quotation marks were returned as verbatim, but spot-check any quote before using it in public copy. it.conex quotes come from the raw HTML and are exact (German, translated).
- Blocked or missing pages: oroinc.com and codelessplatforms.com return 403 to direct fetch (read via the r.jina.ai reader instead). bigcommerce.com/b2b 404 (used /b2b-ecommerce/). rossum.ai/sales-order-processing 404 (used /solutions/order-management/). Esker homepage links to order management were used. williams-commerce.com (hyphen) now serves an unrelated aerospace site; the agency is at williamscommerce.com.
- Microsoft: no marketing landing page for the Business Central Sales Order Agent could be found or fetched (marketplace listing 403, guessed microsoft.com URLs 404). The Microsoft Learn overview was used instead. It is feature documentation, not problem-framed marketing, so treat Microsoft as "positioned by capability, not by pain".
- Aphix Software is now "part of Experlogix"; the Aphix homepage is an acquisition notice, so the Experlogix Digital Commerce page was used for pains.

Theme codes used in the tables (defined in section 2): MAN manual entry, ERR errors, PRICE pricing complexity, REP sales reps tied up, SELF 24/7 self-service, QUOTE quotes, SCALE scale without hiring, CX customer experience/modernise, STOCK stock visibility, EDI, CASH payments/cash, SYNC integration/data out of sync, IT implementation/IT burden, REV revenue growth, SPEED delays/backlogs, STAFF talent/peak/key-person, CHAN many order channels.

## 5. White space (problems nobody leads with)

1. **Wrong prices and margin leakage.** Eight name pricing complexity, but as a feature need ("support contract pricing"), not as a cost ("you invoice the wrong price, you give away margin, you issue credit notes"). No one quantifies it. Workist's "Margin-killing errors" is the only margin framing.
2. **Customer part codes, units and pack sizes.** No competitor names these as a business problem. This is exactly B2Bware's "messy data" root cause, and it is uncontested in the language, though its consequences (errors, delays) are well covered.
3. **Cost of an error after it ships.** Errors are claimed ("98% fewer") but no one names returns, re-deliveries, credit notes or disputes as the pain.
4. **Key-person risk.** Only Conexiom ("Expertise is walking out the door") and it.conex (staff absences) name it; nobody leads. "Orders stop when Sandra is on holiday" is unclaimed.
5. **Your existing connector is broken.** DCKAP ("Failed transactions that nobody sees") and Patchworks ("Silent failures") name it; nobody leads with "fix what you already bought". Every portal assumes a fresh platform.
6. **Customer mandates / digital trading pressure from big customers.** EDI is mentioned by 5, framed as a tech burden; no one frames "your largest customer is forcing you to go digital".
7. **Sales reps as order-takers.** Seven list it; none lead. Workist's "No time for sales" is the sharpest line.
8. **Quotes.** Nine support quotes, none lead (Lleverage's use-case page comes closest).

## 6. Differences by camp and region

**By camp**
- Portals (15): mixed leads. Growth and buyer experience language (sell more, modernise, DTC-like self-serve) is almost exclusive to this camp. Metrics skew to revenue and online adoption. Buyers named: ecommerce managers, sales directors, founders.
- Order capture (7 plus Microsoft): nearly uniform lead on manual order entry, with the richest list of business consequences (staff shortage, burnout, peaks, frustrated customers, missed revenue). Metrics skew to time saved, FTE, error rates, speed. Buyers named: customer service, order desk, sales ops, ERP admins.
- Integration (5): lead on connection, control and implementation speed. Business consequences are thin; IT and ops are the named buyers. Williams (agency) is the exception, leading on growth.

**By region** (HQ from general knowledge, not verified in this pass; small samples)
- DACH (Workist, Turian, it.conex): the only camp to name **labour**: talent shortage, burnout, staff absences, growth without hiring. it.conex (SME, regional) stresses errors, relief for staff and resilience, and gives no metrics.
- UK/IE (SparkLayer, GOb2b, Aphix, Patchworks, Codeless, Williams): plainer "sell more, reduce costs, connect systems" language; SparkLayer is the only UK player naming the inbox as the problem. Few hard metrics outside SparkLayer.
- North America (Conexiom, OrderEase, k-ecommerce, commercebuild, Shopify, DCKAP, WizCommerce, CIMcloud, BigCommerce, Celigo, Oro, B2B Wave): heaviest on quantified metrics (98% fewer errors, 20+ hours per rep, 391% ROI) and on scale and trust/relationship framing.
- Benelux/other EU (Sana, Lleverage, Alumio, Rossum, Esker): Sana and Lleverage are closest to the manufacturer-plus-ERP story; Esker frames it as customer service and cash (CFO office).
