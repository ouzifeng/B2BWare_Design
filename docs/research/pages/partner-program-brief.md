## Partner types

Three ways in, one partnership. Pick the one that matches how you already work. [LP]

| Type | Who | What they do | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implementation partner | Agencies and integrators who run the client relationship | Add order automation to their scope, price and deliver the implementation under their own name; invoice their own work at their own rates | [LP], [PA] 4.3, [DK] slide 7 "each contracts, delivers and supports its own scope" |
| ERP partner / reseller | Resellers and VARs, on any ERP | Give clients a direct answer to "can you automate this?"; a new revenue line without touching core margin; may sell under own brand (white label) | [LP], [PA] 4.2 |
| Referral partner | Consultants who spot the need but do not run the sale | Make the introduction; B2Bware qualifies, demos, contracts, delivers and supports; no delivery obligation | [LP], [DK] slide 7, [PA] 4.1 |

A partner can hold more than one role. [PA] 4.1 (structural, safe to say).

## What partners get

- Recurring commission on every account they bring. Wording on page: "recurring commission on every account, agreed in your partner agreement". [LP] "earn on every account", [DK] "referral fee may apply once revenue is collected", [OW] wording rule.
- Paid once the client has paid. [DK] slide 7 "Referral fee may apply once revenue is collected".
- No cost to join. [LP] FAQ.
- No quota, no exclusivity, no requirement to resell. [DK] slide 7 principles, [PA] non-exclusive appointment.
- Neither party owns the other's delivery; clear ownership. [DK] slides 6 and 7.
- Sales support: joint discovery calls, proposal support, technical scoping. [LP]; [DK] slide 7 "B2Bware qualifies, demos, scopes commercially".
- Product information, demo access, sales material. [PA] 6 (B2Bware obligations).
- A named partner manager. [LP] FAQ; [PR] (partner manager role exists).
- White label for qualified partners: brand the platform as your own. [LP] FAQ; [PR] (independents white-label); [PA] 12 (own brand only where agreed).

## How partners earn

- Recurring commission on accounts they bring, agreed in the partner agreement. [OW], [LP]
- Implementation partners also keep their own implementation fees, invoiced at their own rates. [DK] "each contracts, invoices, delivers and supports its own scope"; [PA] 4.3.
- Resellers may set their own customer-facing price. [PA] 4.2 only (draft). Not used on page.

## What the client buys (customer-facing product truth) [OW]

- Fixed price: £5,000 set-up, £300 a month including 1,000 orders.
- Online ordering portal with customer-specific prices synced live to the ERP. Own portal, never Shopify B2B.
- AI that reads emailed, PDF and spreadsheet orders into the ERP after a human check.
- Fixing and maintaining webshop to ERP links.
- Add-ons: WhatsApp and phone orders, punchout, EDI, sales app.
- ERP stays the system of record; nothing posts without a human approving. [GB] section 1.

## Onboarding steps

[LP] "Apply in minutes. Onboarded in days. Paid on every deal you close." Rewritten as:
1. Apply (form; partner team replies within two business days [LP]).
2. Intro call: agree the role that fits and the partner agreement. [DK] slide 9 (validate the model before an agreement).
3. Onboarding: demo access, product information, sales material. [PA] 6.
4. Bring a client: introduce after basic qualification [DK]; B2Bware runs a free consultation on the client's own data and a fixed quote [OW]/site.
5. Client goes live; recurring commission once the client pays. [DK], [LP]

## Ideal partner

- ERP implementers are most of the target list. [GB] 8
- Agencies, ERP resellers/VARs, consultants. [LP]
- Their clients: wholesalers, distributors, manufacturers on an ERP; repeat B2B buyers on negotiated terms; orders by email, PDF, Excel, phone or reps; per-customer prices, catalogues, terms. [DK] slide 3, [LP]
- Triggers: new ERP project with a portal requirement; replacing a custom or legacy portal that depends on one developer; orders mostly by email/phone/PDF. [DK] slides 2-3
- Want margin, not a moonshot; sell proof, not promises. [LP]

## What partners worry about

- They think like ERP integrators and probe the ERP connection. [GB] 8
- The real risk is the commerce layer: product codes, configurability, per-customer pricing. [GB] 8
- Reputational risk of recommending a vendor; want clear ownership and support boundaries; standard vs custom connector work. [DB] sections 6, 7, 9
- Answer on page: we own the commerce layer (customer prices, customers' own part codes, packs and units, emailed orders) and fix the ERP link when the ERP changes; configured products and bespoke ERPs with no interface are qualified out honestly. [GB] sections 2, 8; site copy.
