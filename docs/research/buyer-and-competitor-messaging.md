# Buyer view and competitor messaging (for the final report)

Captured 23 Sep 2026 from 15 competitor homepages. David asked for this to go into the final report: it is thinking the company has not done before and it shows how to stand apart.

## What the market says

| Camp | Who | Lead message | How it feels to our buyer |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2B shop platforms | Sana, OroCommerce, k-eCommerce, SparkLayer, B2B Wave, GOb2b | "The B2B ecommerce platform", "Built for B2B, powered by ERP" | A software project I'd have to run. Big, expensive, IT-led |
| AI order agents | Workist, Turian, Lleverage, Conexiom | "Automate order entry, inbox to ERP in seconds", "Your autonomous back office" | Impressive but hype-heavy. Will it replace my team? Will it cope with our messy orders? |
| Integration platforms | Patchworks, Celigo, Codeless | "Connect your tech stack", "Connect anything to anything" | Written for IT, not for me. Where SyncSpider used to sit |

Best lines in the set, both in buyer language:
- Sana: "The webstore manufacturing buyers trust, no manual intervention needed" (subline names negotiated pricing, deep catalogues, live stock).
- SparkLayer: "Nothing to rip out. Nothing to retype."

Proof across the category is scale: SparkLayer 3,500 brands, Patchworks 1,700 businesses, Conexiom 1.5B line items a year, Workist 200+ companies (Liebherr, Assa Abloy). B2Bware cannot win on scale and should not try.

## What the buyer is thinking

Ops or sales director, 60-person fixings distributor on Sage 200:
- "Our data's a mess. Contract prices, their part codes, packs. Does anyone actually get that?"
- "I don't want a year-long IT project. I've been burned before."
- "Will it work with our Sage, not Sage in general?"
- "Is AI going to spook my staff, or fall over on our weird orders?"
- "Who are these people, and have they done it for someone like me?"

Competitors answer "what is our software". None answers "do you understand my mess, and will you deal with it for me?"

## Where B2Bware stands apart

1. Say the mess out loud. Every ERP is messy; naming it shows understanding.
2. Done for you, not do it yourself. Fixed price, then run it. Answers "I don't want a project".
3. Talk like the trade: contract bands, their part codes, boxes of 100, accounts on stop.
4. Close, not big: named team, customers you can phone, a free check on their own data.
5. Stay out of the AI race: AI is how some of the work gets done, not the headline.

Target feeling: "These people understand how messy our setup is, they'll sort it for us, and it won't turn into a big project."

## Language: "B2B commerce" and capabilities

- "B2B commerce" is vendor and analyst language. The buyer says "trade customers", "ordering online", "our trade portal", "web orders", "EDI", "order entry". Keep "B2B" for SEO, meta and PPC, not the headline.
- Capabilities (portal, pricing, reorders, approvals, rep ordering, email orders) matter as layer two: shown as answers to the buyer's situation ("let trade customers order online with their own prices"), not as a feature list. Layer one is the problem and the promise.
